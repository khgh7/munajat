Glossary

Common terms in the prayers, commentary and reference notes.

Adab

Proper manners or conduct; in this book, the respectful manner of approaching worship and supplication.

ʿAfiyah

Well-being, safety and protection from harm or affliction; the intended sense depends on the prayer.

Akhirah

The Hereafter: the life to come after death.

Alim / Ulama

A scholar of Islamic learning; ulama is the plural.

Allah

The Arabic name for God.

Amin / Amen

An appeal that Allah accept the prayer.

ʿAsr

The afternoon obligatory prayer.

Ayah

A verse of the Qur’an; plural, ayat.

Barakah

Blessing: goodness, increase and benefit granted by Allah.

Bismillah

The invocation “In the name of Allah.” The full form at the openings adds “the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.”

Companion / Sahabi

A person who met the Prophet as a believer and died as a Muslim.

Daʿif

Weak: a hadith grading indicating that the transmission does not meet the requirements of an accepted sound or fair report. It is a judgment about the report, not a translation of its meaning.

Dajjal

The false messiah whose trial is mentioned in the hadith prayers.

Dhikr

Remembrance of Allah, including praise, glorification and prescribed invocations.

Duʿa

Supplication: calling upon Allah with praise, need, hope or a request. The Urdu plural “duas” refers to individual supplications.

Durood / Salawat

Words asking Allah to send blessings upon the Prophet Muhammad. Durood is the commonly used South Asian term.

Fajr

The dawn obligatory prayer.

Fitnah

A trial, temptation or ordeal; its precise meaning depends on context.

Gharib

A report described as having a lone or unusual route of transmission at some point in its chain. The term alone does not mean that a report is weak; read the accompanying assessment.

Hadith

A transmitted report, usually concerning the Prophet’s words, actions or approval. A report’s attribution and reliability require separate assessment.

Hakim al-Ummah

An honorific meaning “sage of the community,” used for Mawlana Ashraf Ali Thanawi in the source.

Hasan

Fair or good: a positive hadith grading, with criteria that differ in detail among scholars.

Iman

Faith or belief.

Isnad

The chain of transmitters through whom a report is related.

Istighfar

Seeking Allah’s forgiveness.

Jannah

Paradise.

Jumʿah

Friday; also the congregational Friday prayer.

Juz

One of the thirty reading divisions of the Qur’an.

Khanqah

A place associated with spiritual instruction and devotional practice in the South Asian tradition.

Makhdum al-Ulama

An honorific used in the foreword for a respected religious figure honoured by scholars. It is retained as a title.

Manzil

A portion or stage. In this collection it means one of the seven daily reading portions.

Marfuʿ

A report attributed to the Prophet. This describes its attribution, not automatically its authenticity.

Matn

The wording or main text of a transmitted report, as distinct from its chain.

Matruk

Abandoned: a severe criticism of a transmitter or report. The relevant authority is named in the reference notes.

Mawduʿ

Fabricated: a severe critical judgment that a report has been falsely attributed. In this edition such a judgment is always attributed to its source.

Mawlana / Maulana

An honorific commonly used for a respected Muslim scholar or religious teacher.

Mawquf

A report that stops at a Companion rather than being attributed as the Prophet’s own words or action.

Muʿdal

A disconnected report with at least two successive transmitters omitted from its chain.

Mufti

A scholar qualified to give a formal opinion on a question of Islamic law.

Munajat

Intimate supplication or prayer addressed to Allah. “Maqbool” means accepted.

Munkar

Rejected or objectionable: a hadith-critical term used for a seriously problematic report, with technical usage varying by scholar.

Mursal

A report in which a Successor relates directly from the Prophet without naming the Companion link. The missing link is relevant to grading.

Nafs

The self or soul; in some contexts, the lower desires of the self.

Qadi

A judge; it can also appear as an honorific in a person’s name.

Qiblah

The direction faced in the ritual prayer, towards the Kaʿbah in Makkah.

Qunut

A standing supplication in ritual prayer; the exact form and setting vary.

Rizq

Provision or sustenance granted by Allah.

Sadaqah jariyah

An ongoing charity whose benefit continues.

Sahih

Sound or authentic: a positive grading of a report or chain according to the named authority’s criteria.

Sajdah / Sujud

Prostration.

Salah

The prescribed ritual prayer. It is distinct from the broader act of duʿā.

Salam

Peace; also the greeting or words of peace offered to another.

Shadh

Anomalous: a report or wording judged to conflict with stronger transmission. The reference note identifies the scholar making that judgment.

Shirk

Associating partners with Allah.

Sunnah

The Prophet’s teaching and practice; in legal discussion the term can also have a more specific sense.

Surah

A chapter of the Qur’an.

Taqwa

God-consciousness expressed in reverence, obedience and avoidance of wrongdoing.

Tashahhud

The testimony and associated words recited while seated in ritual prayer.

Tawakkul

Reliance upon Allah.

Tawbah

Repentance: turning back to Allah from wrongdoing.

Ummah

The community; in this book, usually the Muslim community.

Witr

The odd-numbered prayer associated with the close of the night prayers.

Wudu

The ritual washing performed before prayer.

Zakah

The obligatory charitable payment due on qualifying wealth.
