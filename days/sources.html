Reference review

Sources, wording and attribution

Scope of the review

All 207 separately numbered entries have been checked for identifiable Qur’anic or hadith sources. The Arabic in the cited source was compared with the collection; longer combinations were checked by component. The review records where the wording is an excerpt, a variant, a combination, or still partly untraced. It also checks the main opening and closing claims.

What a citation establishes

A reference records where a text is transmitted. Authenticity is a separate question. The grades below report the assessments found in the named sources and editions; they are not a fresh independent evaluation of every narrator and chain. Where a grade was not established, that is stated.

Primary texts and numbering

Qur’anic references use surah and verse. Hadith references use the collection and the displayed report number, with a volume/page or alternate locator where needed. Numbering differs between editions. A Dorar link may reproduce a primary collection under its “original sources” section and name the scholar whose grading is quoted. Sunnah.com and the cited digital libraries are access points to those texts.

Unresolved wording and source interpretation

An unverified phrase has not been given an invented reference. Similar wording is labelled as a parallel rather than an exact source. The Arabic of the supplied edition is retained unless a transcription error is confirmed against the scan. Bracketed English clarifications identify supplied meaning where the original Urdu explains an elliptical Arabic phrase.

Reading the original commentary

The translated commentary is preserved as source material. A hadith source listed for the main prayer does not automatically verify every historical anecdote, theological explanation or devotional recommendation in its commentary. Specific attributions, fixed rewards and contextual problems encountered in the review are noted.

Review date

Sources were checked for this edition on 21 October 2026. The source links and named judgments make the review inspectable and allow later corrections to be made transparently.

Opening and closing references

These notes cover material outside the numbered duʿās.

G1 · Opening praise, weekly arrangement and blessings

The repeated opening is the collection’s introductory invocation. No separate Prophetic transmission for its complete wording was established. Its phrase about nearness to Allah and the Messenger’s prayers echoes Qur’an 9:99, but the whole opening is not a Qur’anic verse. The Saturday–Friday sequence is the compiler’s reading plan. The instruction to begin with praise and blessings has support in Abu Dawud 1481; that report does not establish every wording, count or weekly schedule printed in this collection.

Qur’an 9:99 — phrase only

Sunan Abi Dawud 1481 — sahih, al-Albani

Qur’an 33:56 — general command to send blessings

G2 · The foreword’s quotations about duʿā

“Call upon Me; I will respond to you” is the opening command in Qur’an 40:60. The other quotation, “Supplication is the believer’s weapon,” occurs in a longer report from ʿAli in Abu Yaʿla 439, al-Hakim’s al-Mustadrak and al-Qudaʿi’s Musnad al-Shihab 143. Al-Hakim accepted the report, but al-Albani graded it fabricated in al-Silsilah al-Daʿifah 179. This disagreement is material: the foreword’s quotation must not be treated as an undisputed authentic hadith. The general value of duʿā is independently supported by the Qur’anic verse. The statement that duʿā is the essence of worship corresponds to Tirmidhi 3371, which the cited Darussalam edition grades weak. This specific wording should therefore also be distinguished from the general, independently established importance of supplication.

Qur’an 40:60

Al-Hakim, al-Mustadrak 1836 in the linked edition; also numbered 1812 — accepted by al-Hakim

Al-Albani, al-Silsilah al-Daʿifah 179 — fabricated

Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi 3371 — daʿif, Darussalam edition

G3 · Closing Durood and the eighty-year reward

The original Monday note attributes forgiveness of eighty years of sins and the reward of eighty years of worship to eighty recitations after Friday ʿAsr. Al-Sakhawi records this wording from Ibn Bashkuwal in al-Qawl al-Badiʿ, pp. 198–199 of the Dar al-Rayyan edition. In the same discussion he calls related Abu Hurayrah chains weak. He also records that al-ʿIraqi accepted a related version, while expressing reservation about that judgment. The variants and their chains must not be collapsed into one grading. This review has not established the exact fixed reward as sound. The note is retained as original source material, with this qualification. General salawat remains supported by Qur’an 33:56 and Abu Dawud 1481.

Al-Sakhawi, al-Qawl al-Badiʿ, Dar al-Rayyan edition, p. 198

Al-Sakhawi, al-Qawl al-Badiʿ, same edition, p. 199

Qur’an 33:56

Sunan Abi Dawud 1481 — sahih, al-Albani

G4 · Poetry, devotional directions and the final appeal

The opening Urdu poem, Saturday’s House of Allah reflection, Tuesday’s Green Dome couplet and Friday’s closing appeal are translated from the supplied edition. They are identified as poetry, editorial directions or a personal appeal. They are not additional numbered Prophetic prayers. In particular, Saturday’s direction to hold the image of the House of Allah in mind is preserved as the source editor’s devotional instruction; no specific Prophetic attribution for that instruction has been established in this review.

Edition record

Source edition

Prepared from the supplied 136-page scan of Munajat-e-Maqbool. The foreword and final appeal are signed by Hafiz Fazlur Rahim, Deputy Head of Jamia Ashrafia, Lahore. No publication date has been inferred from the scan.

Completeness

Seven daily portions; 207 separately presented prayer entries; the repeated opening praise; closing Durood; translated original commentary, introductory poem and foreword, closing reflections and appeal. The publisher’s decorative covers have not been reproduced as devotional text.

Reference practice

Every numbered entry has a source-review record, including entries whose wording or attribution is only partly established. Source locators, component coverage and named gradings distinguish what was verified from what remains unresolved.

Typography

Arabic: AlQuran IndoPak by QuranWBW, version 4.2.1-WL, matching the approved Durood reference. English: EB Garamond. The text is embedded as vector type for crisp viewing and printing.

Original pages and new pages

The footers of the daily portions retain the original printed-page locators. The centred page numbers, contents and indexes refer to this complete edition.

Review and corrections
