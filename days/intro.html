Munajat-e-Maqbool

Complete Arabic & English

Reference-reviewed edition

Compiled by Mawlana Ashraf Ali Thanawi

Arabic in the approved Indo-Pak typeface, with English translations of the prayers, commentary and original introductory material. With a glossary, topic index, numbered duʿā index and a reference review for every entry.

About this edition

The complete weekly collection

This edition brings together all seven daily portions in the order of the supplied Urdu book: Saturday through Friday. It preserves the Arabic, presents the Urdu prayers and commentary in English, and adds a reference review for every numbered entry. The original foreword, opening poem, closing reflections and final appeal are included.

What has been added

The supplied book has a contents page for the seven portions, but no glossary or detailed index of individual prayers. This edition adds an English contents page, a glossary, an index by topic, a numbered duʿā index, and a linked reference section. Short thematic titles in the indexes are editorial aids.

Arabic and translation

The approved AlQuran IndoPak typeface is retained as embedded, selectable vector text. The English follows the supplied Urdu edition; it is not presented as a new critical translation of every hadith. Where an English interpretation goes beyond the literal Arabic, the reference notes identify the issue. The small qāf and qāf–fāʾ signs in the Arabic are the source’s reading annotations, not additional words of a Qur’anic verse.

Original commentary and new review notes

Boxes headed ORIGINAL COMMENTARY or ORIGINAL SOURCE NOTE translate the source book. They may contain devotional interpretations, historical anecdotes or attributions that should be read alongside the new reference notes. An attribution in the original foreword or commentary is not, by itself, a finding of this review.

A source for every entry

There are 207 separately presented entries: numbers 1–203, with the additional parts 193(a), 193(b), 203(a) and 203(b). Each has a reference-review entry. A source citation identifies a text or a reported transmission; it does not automatically establish that transmission as authentic. Untraced wording, differing readings, weak reports and disputed judgments are identified explicitly.

A guide to reading and sources

The weekly arrangement

The compiler’s reading plan begins on Saturday and ends on Friday, with one manzil each day. This is the collection’s arrangement. It is not presented here as a separately authenticated Prophetic requirement. The opening praise and closing Durood remain with each daily portion. See general reference G1.

Finding a prayer or its source

Use the contents to open a day, the topic index to find a theme, or the numbered index to locate a particular duʿā. Every supplication heading has a source-page pointer. In the PDF, the pointer opens its reference entry; the reference entry links back to the prayer. External source links open the cited text.

Understanding the reference labels

Exact means the cited wording matches apart from orthography and vocalisation. Excerpt means only part of a longer report is printed. Variant means a related transmission differs in wording. Component means the citation supports only the stated portion of a combined prayer. Parallel only means it supports a similar expression, not the exact wording printed here.

Reading a grading carefully

Grades are attributed to a named scholar, collection or edition. Where assessments differ, the difference is recorded. A sound component does not make a whole composite prayer a single sound hadith. “Not verified” means this review did not establish the attribution; it is not proof that no source exists.

Special occasions and rewards

A general encouragement to make duʿā or send blessings does not establish every prescribed count, time or promised reward. In particular, the eighty-year reward attached to the closing Durood is not presented as an established sound promise; see G3 and the note beside Monday’s closing Durood. The limb-by-limb ablution attributions in 151–155 also require the qualifications in their source entries. Friday’s claim about eight names written on the sun (191) is also qualified directly beneath its original commentary.

Source numbering

Original numbering is retained. The unprinted number between 193(b) and 195 is supplied as 194* for navigation. Commentary 15 is placed with prayer 16; commentary 19 also discusses prayer 18. Monday’s commentary 113 accompanies the Ta’if passage within prayer 112. Wednesday’s commentaries 162 and 163 accompany prayers 163 and 164 respectively. These source irregularities do not represent missing prayers.

Foreword to the original edition

Translated from source PDF pages 3–6 (printed pages 4–7).

The following is the original foreword in translation. Its first-person statements, prayers for named people and references to a recent death belong to the historical source. Its general claim of Prophetic attribution must be read with the individual source notes in this edition. The quotations and statements about duʿā are reviewed in G2.

In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful.

All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all worlds. Blessings and peace be upon the leader of the prophets and messengers.

Dear readers, this beautiful collection of prayers, Munajat-e-Maqbool, was selected by Hakim al-Ummah, Mawlana Ashraf Ali Thanawi—may Allah fill his resting place with light—from the prayers of the Qur’an and hadith.

Mawlana Thanawi divided these prayers into seven portions so that those who read them may have the good fortune of reciting one manzil each day. He writes: “These prayers are words spoken by Allah, the Most Generous, and by the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. There should therefore be no hesitation or doubt concerning their acceptance, provided that the person reciting them asks Allah with sincerity, attention and firm conviction.”

While reading these prayers, keep in mind this command of Allah in the Noble Qur’an:

“Call upon Me; I will respond to you.” — Qur’an 40:60

And this saying attributed to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Supplication is the believer’s weapon.” Duʿā is the essence of worship and the best means of seeking Allah’s pleasure.

If these matters are kept in view, I am confident that the person who reads these prayers with a sincere heart will not be left deprived, Allah willing.

The accuracy and careful presentation of this Munajat-e-Maqbool are the fruit of the diligent work of Mawlana Musharraf Ali Thanawi, Qadi Muhammad Yunus Anwar, Qari Ilyas and Mawlana Khalil Ahmad Thanawi—may Allah preserve them. This work has produced a copy corresponding to the one that Mawlana Thanawi, may Allah have mercy on him, kept under study for years. May Allah grant all these respected people the finest reward.

Its beautiful printing and careful presentation are due to the special attention of the late Haji Ghulam Sarwar, an authorised spiritual successor of Mawlana Shah Abrar al-Haq, may Allah have mercy on him. May Allah make his grave a garden of Paradise and make this Munajat-e-Maqbool an ongoing charity for him.

I also request readers to pray for the blessings of both worlds for my very sincere friend Dr Muhammad Zubair and his family. He took a special interest in the printing of this book. I request prayers for forgiveness and pardon for my very sincere friend, the late Lieutenant General Jahangir Nasrullah, who has recently passed away.

These few humble lines were written at Khanqah Sayyid Ahmad Shaheed, may Allah have mercy on him, in the presence of Makhdum al-Ulama, Mawlana Sayyid Nafees al-Husayni Shah. He honoured me with his prayers and encouraged me. May Allah, exalted is His glory, preserve his blessed presence among us for many years with health and well-being, and enable us all to benefit from him. Amen.

Finally, with the utmost humility, I ask you kindly to pray for a good ending and success in both worlds for me and my family.

May Allah reward you with a good reward.

In need of your prayers,
The humble Hafiz Fazlur Rahim, may Allah preserve him
Deputy Head, Jamia Ashrafia, Lahore

A prayer to the Lord of all worlds

The original Urdu opening poem, in English

O holy God, Most Merciful, Most Compassionate,
Fulfiller of needs, Bestower of gifts, Most Generous.

O Lord of all worlds, free of every need,
Set right our affairs in religion and in this world.

You are the Giver, ever ready to give;
The doors of Your mercy stand open at every moment.

Whoever stretches out a hand at Your door
Receives the fulfilment of their desire.

Asking is our task; You have laid it upon us.
You have also taught us the manners of presenting our request.

You commanded us to ask,
And also showed us how to ask.

Indeed, O Lord, You Yourself have taught us
The content of every petition.

At every moment You are ready to give;
You are displeased with the one who does not ask.

In every condition we are Your petitioners;
We have fallen at Your door, O Allah.

For the sake of Your might and majesty,
For the sake of the Prophet and his family,

Now shower Your mercy upon us;
Accept this prayer and these supplications.

Translated from source PDF page 7. This is devotional poetry from the original edition; it is not presented as a Qur’anic passage or an independently authenticated hadith.
